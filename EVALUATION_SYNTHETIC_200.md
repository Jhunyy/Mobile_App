## Metric definitions

Positive class is SCAM. In strict mode, only SCAM is positive; in warning mode, SCAM and SUSPICIOUS are positive. TP = labeled SCAM predicted positive; FP = labeled SAFE predicted positive; TN = labeled SAFE predicted negative; FN = labeled SCAM predicted negative. Accuracy = (TP + TN) / N; precision = TP / (TP + FP); scam recall = TP / (TP + FN); SAFE recall = TN / (TN + FP); F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall); false-positive rate = FP / (FP + TN); false-negative rate = FN / (FN + TP). Undefined divisions are N/A. Three-way exact accuracy requires SAFE→SAFE and SCAM→SCAM; SUSPICIOUS is counted separately.

## Key findings

- Raw Gemma predicted SCAM for 107/200 messages and LEGITIMATE for 93/200. All 200 outputs claimed HIGH confidence, including 21 SAFE→SCAM and 14 SCAM→LEGITIMATE errors. This confidence is model text, not a calibrated probability.
- Of the 21 raw Gemma false positives, production returned SAFE for 17 and SUSPICIOUS for 4. Examples include ordinary Cebuano questions and family messages, names, and a laugh message. Reasons frequently invented OTP theft or phishing where the SMS contained no such request. The four remaining SAFE warnings were a login alert, two OTP delivery messages that said not to share the code, and a bank-card notification directing the recipient to the official hotline.
- Production warned on 94/100 intended scams, including 8/14 raw Gemma false negatives that rules/fusion raised to SUSPICIOUS. Six intended scams became SAFE. The rule verdict was SAFE on 199/200 rows and SUSPICIOUS on one; its nonzero score on some missed scams did not guarantee a warning. Production strict SCAM recall was 50/100, so warning recall and definitive classification must be discussed separately.
- The experimental final had exactly the same four SAFE warnings and zero SAFE→SCAM errors as production. Its 23 verdict changes were all on intended SCAM rows: 17 SCAM→SUSPICIOUS and 6 SUSPICIOUS→SAFE. Thus it did not reduce false-positive warnings, while scam warning recall fell from 94/100 to 88/100 and strict SCAM recall fell from 50/100 to 33/100.
- The debug evidence validator identified unsupported Gemma claims in some harmless messages, but its narrow lexical cues missed explicit credential requests such as “Share the six-digit code” and “I need the OTP,” and advance-fee or threat scams where payment alone was deemed ambiguous. This is an observed coverage gap in the debug heuristic. Some Gemma false negatives asserted that bank login, CVV requests, or payment-before-refund were legitimate; those remain raw-model errors rather than evidence-validation errors.
- On this synthetic set, the experimental approach did not outperform production. Do not adopt it as the production decision policy based on this evaluation.

## Limitations and recommendations

- These are synthetic intended labels, not independently verified real-world fraud outcomes. Do not present these rates as field accuracy. The balanced 100/100 prevalence also differs from many real SMS streams, so precision may change substantially in deployment.
- The evidence validator is a limited lexical check. It cannot verify sender identity, website ownership, or claim truth, and it may miss multilingual paraphrases and fraud mechanisms outside its patterns. Inspect false negatives before any production adoption.
- Review raw Gemma reasons alongside message text for invented links, OTP requests, or urgency; treat self-reported HIGH confidence as uncalibrated. Review SAFE promotional and service rows separately from ordinary conversations.
- Compare on another held-out dataset before changing production thresholds or rules. Record language and scam subtype annotations in a future evaluation set to locate systematic weaknesses without changing this dataset.
